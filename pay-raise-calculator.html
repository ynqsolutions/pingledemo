How a Raise Affects Your Pay

A raise, whether it's a percentage merit increase or a flat cost-of-living adjustment (COLA), changes your annual salary and, in turn, how much shows up in each paycheck. The math is simple, but it's easy to lose track of once taxes, pay frequency, and rounding get involved. This calculator does the arithmetic instantly so you know exactly what to expect.

Key Points

  • A percentage raise and a flat-dollar raise can produce the same increase, just calculated differently.
  • A COLA is meant to track inflation, a merit raise reflects performance, both work the same mathematically.
  • Your pay frequency (weekly, biweekly, semimonthly, monthly) determines how the annual increase is split up.
  • A higher salary can move part of your income into a higher tax bracket, only that portion is taxed at the higher rate.
  • If a raise or promotion was promised in writing and not honored, that may be worth having reviewed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my new salary after a raise?

Multiply your current salary by the raise percentage, then add that to your current salary. For a flat-dollar raise, just add it directly.

Is a COLA the same as a raise?

Not exactly in purpose, a COLA tracks inflation while a merit raise reflects performance, but both work the same mathematically.

Does a raise change my tax withholding?

It can push part of your income into a higher bracket, but only that portion is taxed at the higher rate.
