How Tip Pooling Math Works

A tip pool combines tips collected over a shift or period and splits them across the staff involved, usually proportional to hours worked, or by a points system that weights different roles differently. The math itself is simple, the total is divided in proportion to each person's hours or points, but doing it by hand for a full roster gets tedious fast. This calculator handles the arithmetic once you tell it how the pool should be split.

Key Points

  • California generally allows tip pooling among employees who provide direct service, like servers, bussers, and bartenders.
  • Owners, managers, and supervisors generally cannot take a share of pooled tips in California.
  • Splitting by hours gives a share proportional to time worked, a points system lets you weight roles differently.
  • This tool doesn't determine whether your specific pool structure or participant list is lawful, only the math once you decide.

What This Calculator Doesn't Do

Frequently Asked Questions

Who can be included in a tip pool in California?

Generally employees who provide service to customers, like servers, bussers, and bartenders, not owners, managers, or supervisors.

Does this calculator check if a tip pool is legal?

No, it only does the math once you decide the split method, it doesn't evaluate legality.

What's the difference between hours and points?

Hours gives a proportional share by time worked, points lets you weight roles differently based on values you assign.
