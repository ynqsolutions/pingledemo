Requesting a Sabbatical

Unlike PTO or sick leave, a sabbatical is generally a discretionary benefit set entirely by your employer's own policy, there's no legal requirement to offer one. A clear, well-planned request that shows you've thought through coverage for your responsibilities gives it the best chance of approval.

Key Points

  • Sabbaticals are generally a discretionary employer benefit, not a legal requirement.
  • Check your employee handbook for any formal sabbatical policy before requesting.
  • A specific purpose and clear dates make the request easier to evaluate.
  • Propose how your responsibilities will be covered while you're away.

What This Template Doesn't Cover

It doesn't guarantee approval, sabbaticals are generally discretionary, and it doesn't address pay, benefits continuation, or job protection during the leave. Those details depend entirely on your employer's specific policy.

TL;DR

A sabbatical is generally a discretionary employer benefit, not a legal right. Check your handbook for a formal policy, and propose clear dates and coverage in your request.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my employer required to offer sabbaticals?

No, sabbaticals are generally a discretionary benefit set by employer policy, not a legal requirement.

Will I still be paid during a sabbatical?

It depends entirely on your employer's specific policy, some are paid, some unpaid, check your handbook.

Is my job protected during a sabbatical?

That also depends on your employer's policy, ask HR directly before finalizing your plans.
