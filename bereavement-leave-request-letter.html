Bereavement Leave in California

California law generally entitles most employees to a period of bereavement leave following the death of a family member, though the specifics depend on your employer's policy and the law's current requirements. A short written notice lets your employer know when your leave began and gives you a record of the request.

Key Points

  • California law generally provides eligible employees with bereavement leave for the death of a qualifying family member.
  • You're not required to share more detail than you're comfortable with.
  • Check your employer's specific policy for how leave is documented or requested.
  • Retaliation for taking legally protected bereavement leave is generally unlawful.

Frequently Asked Questions

Am I entitled to bereavement leave in California?

Most employees are for a qualifying family member's death, though specifics depend on your employer and current law, check your policy.

Do I have to provide documentation?

Your employer may request reasonable documentation, such as a death certificate or obituary, check your specific policy.

Can I be punished for taking bereavement leave?

No, retaliation for taking legally protected bereavement leave is generally unlawful in California.
