Why Put a Resignation in Writing?

California is an state, so nothing legally requires a written resignation letter. But a short, dated letter creates a clear record of your last day and exactly what you did, and didn't, agree to. If a dispute ever comes up later, over final pay, unused PTO, or the circumstances of your departure, having something in writing is worth far more than a verbal conversation nobody wrote down.

Key Points

  • Resigning in writing isn't legally required in California, but it creates a clear, dated record.
  • Resigning never waives wages, overtime, or other amounts you're already owed.
  • There's no legal notice requirement for most employees; two weeks is a common courtesy, not a rule.
  • Check any written contract first, some include their own notice terms.
  • Don't sign anything else your employer hands you at the same time without reading it carefully.

What This Template Does (and Doesn't) Cover

This template gives you a clean, professional way to document your last day and put your resignation in writing. It doesn't address non-compete clauses, severance negotiations, unresolved wage disputes, or a release you may be asked to sign at the same time, those situations are specific enough that a general template can't safely cover them, and are exactly when it's worth having an attorney look at your specific paperwork first.

How Much Notice Do You Have to Give?

For most employees, none, legally speaking. Two weeks is a workplace norm, not a law. Some offer letters or employment contracts specify their own notice period, so check yours before assuming two weeks is either required or sufficient.

TL;DR

A written resignation letter isn't required in California, but it creates a clear record and doesn't waive any wages or other amounts you're owed. Two weeks' notice is customary, not legally required, check your contract first. If a non-compete, unresolved wage claim, or dispute is involved, get it reviewed before you resign.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to resign in writing in California?

No, but it creates a clear record of your last day and what you did and didn't agree to.

Does resigning waive my right to unpaid wages or overtime?

No. Those rights exist independently of how or why your employment ends.

How much notice do I have to give before resigning?

Generally none, legally. Two weeks is customary, but check any written contract first.

Should I sign anything my employer gives me when I resign?

Not without reading it carefully, and not without an attorney's review if a contract, non-compete, or dispute is involved.
