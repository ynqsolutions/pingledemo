Do X-Ray Techs Get Overtime and Meal Breaks in California?

Yes. Radiologic and imaging technologists are almost always entitled to full overtime and break protections.

Free Consultation Contact Us
By Corey A. Pingle, Esq. Employment Law Attorney

August 26, 2026 · 6 min read

TL;DR

Radiologic technologists, MRI techs, and other imaging staff are hourly, non-exempt workers under California law. They're entitled to overtime past 8 hours a day or 40 a week, plus meal and rest breaks. On-call coverage and single-coverage overnight shifts are where problems tend to show up.

X-ray, CT, MRI, and ultrasound technologists keep a hospital moving, often as the only imaging coverage on a shift. That single-coverage reality is exactly what creates so many wage and break problems in this field. We hear from imaging techs across Sacramento, San Bernardino, and San Diego who go entire shifts without a real break because there's simply no one else to cover the department.

01

Overtime for Imaging Technologists

Imaging technologists are protocol- and equipment-driven roles, which puts them squarely in territory under California's for the healthcare industry. Standard overtime rules apply: time and a half past 8 hours in a day or 40 in a week, and double time past 12 hours in a day.

02

On-Call Pay for Overnight and Weekend Coverage

Many facilities keep an imaging tech on call overnight or on weekends rather than staffing a full shift. If you're required to stay within a set response time, say, able to reach the hospital within 20 or 30 minutes, that on-call time can itself be compensable, and any time you're actually called in must be paid, often at a minimum call-back rate on top of the hours actually worked.

03

Breaks on a Single-Coverage Shift

This is the issue we hear about most from imaging techs. When you're the only tech on shift, there's often no one to relieve you for a true, duty-free 30-minute meal break or a 10-minute rest break. Under California's rules, that's the employer's problem to solve through staffing, not something a tech is expected to simply go without. A missed or interrupted break entitles you to one additional hour of pay at your regular rate for each violation, per day.

04

Who This Affects Most

Overnight and weekend imaging techs at smaller hospitals and urgent care imaging centers face the heaviest exposure, since these facilities often run with only one tech per shift. MRI techs are frequently called in for after-hours trauma or stroke protocol scans, generating on-call and call-back pay questions. And traveling and per diem imaging techs working across multiple facilities in Fountain Valley, Beverly Hills, and San Francisco often see inconsistent break and overtime tracking between assignments, which makes it easy for violations to slip through unnoticed.

05

Common Violations

Watch for automatically deducted meal breaks that were never actually taken, unpaid on-call time with a strict response requirement, and call-back shifts paid at your regular rate instead of the correct overtime or minimum call-back rate.

06

What to Do Next

Track your actual shifts, on-call windows, and any breaks that were interrupted or skipped because you were the only coverage. This connects closely to the broader pattern we see in skipped meal break claims and general unpaid wages and overtime cases.
