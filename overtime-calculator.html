California's Overtime Rules

Most non-exempt employees in California earn overtime based on both daily and weekly hours, whichever gives a bigger premium for a given hour. On top of that, working all seven days of a single workweek triggers its own premium for hours on that seventh day, regardless of how many hours were worked the other six days.

Key Points

  • 1.5x pay is generally owed for hours over 8 in a single day, or over 40 in a single week.
  • 2x pay is generally owed for hours over 12 in a single day.
  • Working all seven days of the workweek generally earns 1.5x for the first 8 hours on the seventh day, and 2x after that.

How This Calculator Works

It applies the daily 8/12-hour thresholds to each day you enter, then checks whether your weekly total still tips you into additional overtime under the 40-hour weekly rule. If you mark that you worked all seven days, it also applies the seventh-consecutive-day premium to that day specifically. It doesn't know about an approved alternative workweek schedule, a union contract with its own overtime terms, or whether your specific role is properly classified as exempt.

TL;DR

California generally requires 1.5x pay for hours over 8 in a day or over 40 in a week, and 2x pay for hours over 12 in a day. Working all seven days of the workweek adds its own premium for the seventh day. A salary alone doesn't make you exempt from any of this.

Frequently Asked Questions

When do I earn overtime in California?

Generally 1.5x for hours over 8 in a day or over 40 in a week, and 2x for hours over 12 in a day, plus a seventh-day premium if you worked all seven days of the workweek.

Does being salaried mean I can't get overtime?

No. Exemption depends on your actual job duties and salary level, not simply on being paid a salary instead of hourly.

What if my employer only pays "straight time" for extra hours?

If you're non-exempt, California law generally requires overtime premiums regardless of what your employer calls the extra hours.
